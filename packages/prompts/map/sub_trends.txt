WHAT SYNTHESIS MEANS HERE
The Key Trend is a synthesis. Each sub-trend must also be synthesized — not a direct echo of one thinker's claim, but a pattern that requires at least two claims combined. Before naming any sub-trend, ask:
1. Is this the Key Trend, just smaller? A restatement is not a sub-trend. Zoom into a specific mechanism, actor group, or consequence — do not restate the headline.
2. Could a single source have named this alone? Then it is a summary, not a synthesis.
3. Do they together reveal different LAYERS of the Key Trend? At minimum: one should explain a cause or driver, one should describe a specific observable behavior, one should track a consequence or tension emerging from the shift. If they all describe the same kind of thing, stop and check what layer is missing.
4. No two sub-trends may anchor on the same source, study, or anecdote as their primary evidence. If two of your candidates both need the same study to exist, they are one sub-trend — merge them and find another elsewhere in the evidence, or return one fewer.
5. Two sub-trends that state the same mechanism from opposite valences are one sub-trend. If one candidate's "to" state is another's thesis inverted ("works only when X" / "fails because not-X"), collapse them into the one that carries both sides.
6. Swap test: exchange two candidates' subtitles. If each subtitle still roughly fits the other's name, the two are not distinct — sharpen or merge.

RULES FOR SUB-TREND NAMES
- 1–2 words. Short, intriguing, memorable (NOT "AI Adoption", "Trust Issues", "Changing Behavior"). Alliteration welcome, not required.
- Names feel coined, not described: compound words, portmanteaus, or unexpected two-word pairings that name the specific manifestation, not the category. "Signal Fatigue" names a behavioral response; "Trust Issues" names a topic.
- Do not use the Key Trend's name (or part of it) in a sub-trend name, and no two of your names may share a noun. Each name must be distinguishable from its siblings at a glance — if the subtitle is needed to tell two apart, the names are not distinct enough.
- Do not begin or end a name with a word that already begins or ends a TAKEN name ("Delegated X", "X Erosion", "X Gap" families count as echoes).
- TEST: could this name appear under a completely different Key Trend? Then it is not specific enough. Does it feel described rather than coined? Rename it.

RULES FOR SUBTITLES (mandatory)
- One complete, specific sentence that fully explains what the sub-trend is. Journalist subheading, not marketing copy. Present tense, declarative, no hedging.
- Anchor it in at least one specific: a named company, a named person, a named technology, or a named behavior — not a statistic; the stat has its own space on the page. Name WHAT is specifically happening and WHO is experiencing it.
- TEST: remove the sub-trend name. If the subtitle could describe a sibling or the Key Trend itself, sharpen it.

RULES FOR DESCRIPTIONS
- Exactly 2 sentences. Strict.
- Sentence 1: what is happening — name actual actors, technologies, or measurable behaviors, not "brands are doing X". Sentence 2: what it means for consumers or brands.
- Sentence 2 must name the implication, not restate the observation. If sentence 2 could have been sentence 1 reworded, rewrite it.
- Never use "landscape", "paradigm", "synergy", "ecosystem", "navigate", or "leverage".
