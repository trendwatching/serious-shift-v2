FROM / TO (the spine of the shift)
- `from` = the assumption or arrangement that is ending. `to` = what replaces it.
- One clause each, 12–24 words. They must be a genuine pair: the same variable, moved.
- Direction is not negotiable: `from` is always the state that is ending, `to` is always what replaces it. Before returning, re-read every pair and confirm the arrow of time points from old to new; an inverted pair fails the page.
- Right: from "Literacy treated as a stable baseline citizens reliably have" → to "Cognitive capacity treated as a variable that must be actively maintained, or it falls"
- Wrong: a restatement, a cause/effect pair, or two unrelated observations.

WHATS_CHANGING / WHY_NOW (two panels the reader toggles between)
