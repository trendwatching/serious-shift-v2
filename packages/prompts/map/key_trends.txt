WHAT SYNTHESIS MEANS
Synthesis is not summarizing. It is not finding the most common theme. It is not echoing what one thinker already said. Synthesis means holding two or more thinkers' claims together — including claims that tension or contradict each other — and finding the pattern that none of them named individually.

Before naming any Key Trend, ask yourself:
1. Which two or more thinkers' claims does this trend require? If you removed one thinker's evidence and the trend would still exist, it is probably a summary of that one thinker's view — not a synthesis.
2. Is there disagreement in the evidence? Find the thinkers who agree on what is happening but disagree on what it means, or who see the same shift from opposite value positions. That gap is where the real trend lives.
3. Would any individual thinker recognize this trend as already their own idea? If yes, go deeper. The synthesis should arrive at something no single source quite named.

RULES FOR KEY TREND NAMES
- 1–2 words. Short, intriguing, memorable. The name creates curiosity; the subtitle delivers the meaning. Alliteration works well but is not required.
- Names feel coined, not described. They compress a new behavior or tension into a single concept that didn't exist before: not a headline, not a category, but the name for a thing that now needs a name. Favor compound words, portmanteaus, or unexpected two-word pairings — "Transumers", "Proof Premium", "Autonomy Tax" — over adjective + noun where the adjective is just a modifier ("Enhanced Trust", "New Dynamics", "Digital Shift").
- Name what is changing, not what the domain is about. "Delegated Desire" names a shift (desire is being handed off). "AI Shopping" names a topic. Do not use the domain name in a trend name, and do not use words that could describe any trend anywhere: "Transformation", "Evolution", "Disruption", "Emergence".
- Right: "Synthetic Trust", "Delegated Desire", "Proof Premium", "Silent Commerce"
- Wrong: "AI Changes Consumer Behavior" (descriptive, not a name), "The Rise of Authenticity" (generic), "Trust Issues" (category label)
- The audience is brand strategists and executives, not AI researchers. Name and subtitle must parse for a curious executive on first read. Never build a name on AI-insider jargon: no "Benchmark", "Eval", "Alignment", "Pacing", "Agentic", "Compute", "Open-Source/Open Parity", "Scaffolding", "Inference", "Checkpoint", "Weights", "Frontier" or similar terms of art. The same goes for an economist's vocabulary ("Autarky", "Kaldor", "Baumol" — or any researcher's surname): a name a curious executive would have to search has failed before the subtitle loads. If the shift is about one of those things, name the human or commercial consequence instead.
- Every Key Trend must be distinct from the others — no overlapping trends, and no two of your names sharing a noun. If you have "Trust Proxy" and "Trust Collapse", one is a sub-variant of the other: collapse them or differentiate them.
- TEST: say the name out loud. Could it have been on a PowerPoint slide from 2019? Does it feel described rather than coined? Either way — rename it.

- One complete, specific sentence. Super descriptive: explain exactly what the trend is. The subtitle carries the meaning the name deliberately withholds; if it is vague, the name has failed.
- Write it as a journalist writes a subheading, not as a marketer. Present tense, declarative, no hedging ("may be", "could potentially", "seems to be").

SELECTION — WHAT QUALIFIES AS A KEY TREND
Before anything gets named, it must pass four tests. Failing any one means it is a sub-trend, a domain-level observation, or noise:
1. DOMAIN INTEGRITY — does this trend belong specifically to this domain, or could it sit unchanged in another? A trend that is really about what institutions, governments, or employers are doing does not belong in Consumers, even if consumers are affected. Reject any trend that could be lifted and placed unchanged into a different domain.
2. DECISION-MAKER URGENCY — read the trend as a senior CMO or Innovation Director would. It must carry a commercial or strategic consequence a decision-maker could build a response to, not just be true and interesting. Reject purely descriptive trends.
3. DISTINCTNESS — for each pair of your trends: same underlying cause told at two magnifications is one trend plus a sub-trend. Merge or cut. If cutting one trend would leave no meaningful gap, cut it.
4. KEY TREND vs SUB-TREND — a Key Trend is a broad structural shift several different things could be symptoms of. A specific manifestation of a broader shift already in your list is a sub-trend: demote it.
5. ARENA SPREAD — step back from your full list and name each trend's arena in one word: government, work, commerce, identity, information, money, health. If more than a third of your trends share one arena, the sphere is telling one story several times — a reader meeting five government trends in a row hears "institutions are losing grip" five times, however distinct the mechanisms. Merge the weakest same-arena pair and go find what the evidence says about the arenas you have not covered. A sphere is a map, not a beat.
